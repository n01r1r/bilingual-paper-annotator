# Analysis and fidelity

## Preserve the paper

- Retain every source paragraph in its original section. Preserve established terminology, quantitative statements, mathematical notation, definitions, citation numbering, and references. Retain qualifications, assumptions, limitations, and counterarguments. Keep figures, captions, footnotes, and tables with their source context and references.
- Write complete Korean translations. Preserve logical connectives, causal direction, conditions, modality, and the scope of claims. Use one Korean rendering for each technical concept. Keep an established English term when it is more precise.
- Keep author claims and editorial analysis distinct. Distinguish assumptions, observations, correlations, proposed explanations, causal claims, and demonstrated results. Explain the evidential basis and uncertainty of editorial judgments.

## Annotate each paragraph

**Paragraph function** names the immediate role. Choose the best fit from roles such as problem introduction, background, thesis, definition, method, evidence, inference, counterargument, rebuttal, limitation, implication, or conclusion.

**Logical role** explains what the paragraph establishes, which earlier premise or assumption it needs, what later inference it supports, and how it relates to the thesis. Cite the relevant paragraph IDs when a dependency crosses paragraphs. Focus on actual dependencies and distinguish claims, premises, evidence, inferences, and conclusions.

**Critical commentary** identifies one consequential technique, assumption, limitation, or possible weakness. Check whether the evidence supports the scope of the claim, assumptions are stated and justified, the inference follows, alternatives remain plausible, or the argument depends on unstated conditions. Distinguish a demonstrated flaw from an unresolved concern. If the paragraph offers insufficient grounds for criticism, describe a relevant technique or the boundary of what the paragraph can establish.

Highlight short English passages with a clear argumentative function. Use yellow for the core thesis, red for key concepts, blue for evidence, green for concessions and rebuttals, and purple for methods and assumptions. Use the same categories for card borders and the legend. Choose the card color by the paragraph's predominant role. Use the neutral card style when none of the five categories fits. Keep Korean translation blocks free of color highlights.

## Trace the full argument

- Follow the paper's own structure. Use problem → thesis → evidence → counterargument → conclusion when it fits. Explain what each step establishes, what evidence or assumptions support it, and how it connects to the next step. Represent branches or parallel arguments when the paper uses them. Include counterarguments when the paper addresses them.
- Give the central thesis in one precise sentence in each language. Preserve the author's scope and level of certainty.
- For the strongest argument, state what it establishes, why its support is persuasive, and which conditions make that assessment hold.
- For the weakest argument, state the claim, the gap in its support, and the evidence or analysis that would strengthen it. Explain when the text leaves this comparison indeterminate.

## Write both analyses at the same level of detail

- Apply ASD-STE100-inspired principles to generated English explanations. Use short, direct sentences with explicit subjects, consistent terms, and clear logical relationships. Preserve specialized terminology and the original paper's wording. Describe the writing style as inspired by ASD-STE100 when relevant. Reserve compliance claims for formally verified work.
- Apply the plain-language principles of ISO 24495-1:2023 to Korean. Make content relevant, easy to find, understandable, and usable. Prefer natural academic Korean with clear sentence structures and direct verbs. Preserve necessary qualifications and technical complexity.
- Check equivalence claim by claim across both versions of each annotation and overview item. Include the same evidence, dependencies, caveats, and level of critical detail. Remove generic praise, repeated summaries, vague statements, and unsupported criticism.
